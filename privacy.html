Privacy Policy

WakeBait for iOS · Last updated 29 September 2026

WakeBait is an alarm app that wakes you up with your own memes. This policy explains what information WakeBait uses, where it goes, and what you can do about it. We've kept it short and plain.

The short version

  • Your alarms, sounds and wake stats stay on your phone. We never see them. WakeBait doesn't keep your videos at all, only the sound from them.
  • WakeBait doesn't have accounts. You don't sign in, and we don't collect your name or email.
  • No ads. No tracking you across other apps or websites. We don't sell your data.
  • Apple sells the subscription and takes the payment. A service called RevenueCat checks your subscription for us. It gets a random ID for your copy of WakeBait, not your name or email.
  • Each time you open it, WakeBait fetches its settings and sound list from our server, run by a service called Supabase. That request doesn't say who you are.

Who we are

WakeBait is made by Ryan Ginns in Lennox Head, New South Wales, Australia. In this policy, "we" and "us" means Ryan Ginns. We're responsible for the personal information described here.

For any privacy question or request, email rjginns@gmail.com.

What we collect and why

No account

WakeBait doesn't have accounts. You don't sign in, and we don't collect your name, email address or phone number.

Your subscription

WakeBait's subscription is sold through the App Store.

Apple takes the payment. We never see your card or bank details.

RevenueCat checks your subscription for us. RevenueCat's software runs inside WakeBait. It checks in with RevenueCat each time you open the app, whether or not you subscribe, and when you look at plans, subscribe or restore a purchase. RevenueCat receives:

  • a random ID it creates for your copy of WakeBait. It isn't your name, email or Apple Account.
  • your purchase details from Apple: which plan, the price, when it started, and any trial, renewal, cancellation or restore
  • whether your subscription is active
  • your device type, iOS version and app version
  • your App Store country and language settings
  • your IP address, which RevenueCat uses to work out your country and says it then discards
  • your iPhone's App Tracking Transparency status for WakeBait. WakeBait never asks to track you, so this can only show that you haven't been asked, or that tracking requests are turned off or restricted on your iPhone.
  • when the app last checked in

We use this to:

  • sell the subscription and check it's genuine
  • restore your purchase if you reinstall or change phones
  • unlock WakeBait Pro
  • see overall subscription numbers in RevenueCat's dashboard, such as how many trials become subscriptions

Because WakeBait has no accounts, your RevenueCat ID isn't connected to your name or email.

"Manage subscription" in the app opens Apple's own subscription page. WakeBait sends nothing to us or to RevenueCat when you tap it.

App settings and sounds

Each time you open WakeBait, it asks our server for the app's current settings and the list of sounds it offers, and downloads sound and picture files when it needs them. The server is run for us by Supabase.

The request carries the app's build number. Like any request over the internet, it also carries your IP address and basic details about your device and iOS version. It doesn't include your name, an ID for you, or anything about your alarms, sounds or wake log. We don't use it to identify you. Supabase keeps short-lived request logs, as any web server does.

Things that stay on your phone

Everything in this section is stored only on your iPhone. It's never sent to us or to the services we use. Backups are covered below.

  • Your first name, if you give it. The Home screen uses it to greet you after an alarm.
  • Your alarms. Time, repeat days, sound, and whether each alarm is on.
  • Alarm details for iOS. So your alarm can ring, WakeBait gives iOS the alarm time, snooze length, sound and a title. The title is the sound's name (for your own imports, the name you typed), or "WakeBait" for the standard alarm. iOS shows it on the Lock Screen and in the snooze countdown, so anyone who can see your phone can see it.
  • Your wake log. Each time you stop an alarm or tap "I'm up", WakeBait notes the time, which alarm and sound it was, and how many minutes after the alarm you got up. It powers your wake stats, your streak, the Home greeting and the "Recently used" sort in your Library. It's a record of when you wake up, so we want to be upfront about it. It never leaves your phone.
  • Your onboarding answers. The sound, time and days you picked for your first alarm, which plan you tapped, and whether you allowed alarms. We use them to set up your first alarm.
  • Your settings. Snooze length, haptics, how your Library is sorted and laid out, and a few flags, such as whether you've finished onboarding.
  • Whether you've let WakeBait set alarms.
  • Your imported sounds. See below.

How importing a video works

  • You pick a video in Apple's photo picker. WakeBait only gets the video you choose. It can't see the rest of your photos, and it doesn't ask for access to them.
  • WakeBait works on a temporary copy of that video inside the app. From it, WakeBait keeps up to 30 seconds of audio, a small still image for the cover, the name you type and the shelf you pick.
  • The temporary copy is deleted when you save or cancel the clip. If an import fails, or the app closes part-way through, the copy can stay in the app's temporary storage until iOS clears it. It never leaves your phone.
  • The saved sound and cover are made fresh from the audio and picture alone, so they carry no location or camera details.
  • WakeBait never uploads your videos, sounds or covers. It never connects to TikTok or Instagram. You save videos from those apps yourself.

Backups. If you back up your iPhone to iCloud or a computer, the information above is included, as it is for most apps. Apple holds iCloud backups under your Apple Account. We can't access them.

Permissions we ask for

  • Alarms. This is the only permission prompt WakeBait shows. iOS shows: "WakeBait needs this to ring your alarm with the sound you picked." Without it, WakeBait can't set alarms. You can change it any time in the Settings app, and WakeBait has a button that takes you there.
  • Live Activities. WakeBait shows your snooze countdown on the Lock Screen. iOS doesn't ask about this one, but you can turn Live Activities off for WakeBait in Settings.

WakeBait doesn't ask for your photo library, camera, microphone, location, contacts, calendars, health data, Bluetooth, local network, clipboard or notifications. It doesn't ask to track you, because it doesn't track you.

If you email us

If you email us for help, we'll use your email address and what you tell us only to reply to you, and we'll delete the conversation once it's sorted unless you ask us to keep it.

What never happens

  • No ads, and no advertising networks in the app.
  • No tracking. We don't follow what you do across other companies' apps or websites, and we don't use your device's advertising ID.
  • We don't sell your personal information or share it for advertising.
  • No data brokers.
  • No analytics or crash-reporting tools in the app. The only numbers we look at are the subscription totals in RevenueCat's dashboard, described under "Your subscription". If you've chosen to share analytics with app developers in your iPhone's settings, Apple may send us crash reports and usage statistics. Apple controls that, and you can turn it off in Settings.
  • We never upload your alarms, wake log, videos or sounds.
  • We don't use your information to make automated decisions that significantly affect you.

Where your information is stored

We're based in Australia. RevenueCat stores information in the United States (on Amazon Web Services). Supabase runs our server in the United States (also on Amazon Web Services). Apple stores information as set out in Apple's policy. This means your information may be stored outside your country. RevenueCat and Supabase protect it under their agreements with us.

How long we keep it

  • On your phone: until you delete it. You can delete sounds and alarms any time. Deleting the app removes the rest, including your wake log. There's no separate button to clear the wake log yet.
  • Subscription records at RevenueCat: for as long as we use RevenueCat to run WakeBait's subscription, or sooner if you ask us to delete them. Apple keeps its own record of your purchase.
  • Request logs at Supabase: a short time, as part of Supabase's standard logging.
  • Support emails: until your question is sorted, unless you ask us to keep them.
  • Backups: Apple keeps these according to your backup settings.
